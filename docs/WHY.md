# Why For Three

*Words by Jingyi. v0, August 2026 — this will keep changing, and that's the point.*

---

I'm a data scientist. I use AI every day, well enough that it turned into
real output, and real career capital. I can sit in a room full of engineers
and follow the conversation.

But I can't do what they do.

Chinese has a precise phrase for my condition: 知其然而不知其所以然 — I know
*that* it works, without knowing *why*. Day to day, this blocks nothing. AI
executes for me; I ship things I couldn't have built alone. That's exactly
what makes the gap invisible — and exactly what makes it dangerous. Because
the *why* is what lets you judge: is this output right? Is this approach
sane? Should I push back? Without it, I can understand everything and
evaluate nothing.

Here's the sentence I keep coming back to:

**AI sets your floor. Understanding sets your ceiling.**

Anyone can now do more than they understand — that's the floor rising, and
it's genuinely new. But how far you can direct, judge, and build stops
exactly at the depth you actually possess. Ability in the AI era climbs a
three-step ladder: **understanding → judgment → creation**. AI parks
everyone comfortably on step one. The scarce step — the one you can't
outsource, can't prompt your way into — is judgment.

## Why not just take a course?

Because everything shaped like school fails outside of school, and it fails
structurally, not because adults are lazy. Six years of Harvard/MIT MOOC
data ([Reich & Ruipérez-Valiente, *Science*
2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30630920/)): the vast majority of
learners never return after their first year. Textbooks produce knowledge
that evaporates. LeetCode produces skill shaped exactly like LeetCode.
Duolingo — which I respect — produces a habit, and habit is not nothing:
out of its enormous retained base, some people really do end up speaking to
the world. But it optimizes the streak, not the capability, and it has no
concept of graduating.

The common root: after school, learning gets divorced from use. School
force-bound them together with immersion, deadlines, and people watching.
That structure is gone, and content — now free and infinite — was never the
scarce part. **Structure is scarce: sequence, feedback, an ending, and an
honest definition of "done."**

One more thing my own history taught me. I learned AI with no manual —
use first, understand after, judge eventually. That order is the design:
knowledge must be derived from use, never pushed ahead of it.

## The method is in the name

举一反三 — from the Analects: shown one corner, return with three. Confucius
meant it as a rule of teaching: the student's own inference does the work,
or nothing does. Modern learning science agrees from every angle — what you
retrieve, generate, and extend yourself is what you keep.

So For Three teaches with minimal corners and demands the other three:
drills that resurface your own mistakes, concepts anchored to what you
already know (SQL, in my case), real AI-agent transcripts as reading
material, and a capstone that ships a tool for your actual life. Your life
is the curriculum. Transfer isn't a hoped-for outcome; it's the mechanism.

## If this ever grows

Then it grows tiered — 宽进严出, wide in, strict out by choice. A wide
layer that offers habit and fun and asks little: commercially honest, and
an honorable funnel — the wider the base, the more people cross over. And a
protected strict layer for anyone with the ability and the will: real
difficulty, real assessment, real evidence. Duolingo is wide-in, no-exit.
School is narrow-in, strict-out. I want wide-in, opt-in strict-out.

I'll say the risk out loud: "people want real capability" may be a minority
demand. Most of the market wants credentials and the feeling of progress.
Fine — the strict layer's evidence portfolio *is* a credential, just one
that happens to be true.

## Where this lands (v0)

- **Mission:** Fill in the why, for people out of school — upgrade
  understanding into judgment, and judgment into creation.
  帮离开学校的普通人补上"所以然"——把理解升级为判断,把判断升级为创造。
- **Vision:** A school of one, for anyone entering any field — sequenced,
  finite, evidenced, enjoyable. CS literacy is the first department, not
  the product.
- **First user:** me. The course exists because I need it. If it works on
  me, it earns the right to a second user.

## Appendix: how this binds engineering decisions

For the build agents. Every one of these traces back to the essay above:

- **Deterministic-first grading** — trust in feedback is a precondition for
  training judgment.
- **Finite, semester-like, graduation-shaped** — endings are
  learning-optimal even when infinite feeds are retention-optimal.
- **Evidence portfolio over completion certificates.**
- **Analogy + precise term** content format — on-ramps for understanding,
  exact language for judgment.
- **Core/elective + skip-debt** — 宽进严出 in miniature, already shipped.
- **Subject-agnostic schema** — the ladder is not a CS-only phenomenon.
